\begin{abstract}
\noindent I run two artificial-intelligence scenarios through OG-UK, the UK calibration of the OG-Core overlapping-generations model, with UN demographics, eighty annual cohorts and a PolicyEngine-calibrated tax system. Both scenarios raise the capital exponent of a Cobb-Douglas production function from \genGammaBase{} to \genGammaShocked{} between \genStartYear{} and \genEndYear{}, a \genLabourShareFallPp{} percentage-point fall in the labour share, and differ only in the productivity term solved alongside it: ``automation only'' holds output at fixed inputs unchanged --- a zero technology wedge, motivated by but not equivalent to the Office for Budget Responsibility's March~2026 technological-displacement scenario, which targets an unchanged \emph{level} of GDP --- while ``automation and productivity'' delivers the \genTfpGainPct{} per cent measured total-factor-productivity gain of \citet{korinek2026}. By \genEndYear{}, GDP is \genAnthGdpGapThirtyAbs{} per cent and \genZeroTfpGdpGapThirtyAbs{} per cent above the no-AI path. The gains accrue through capital: the capital stock rises \genAnthCapitalGapThirtyAbs{} and \genZeroTfpCapitalGapThirtyAbs{} per cent, while the real wage falls \genAnthWageGapThirtyAbs{} and \genZeroTfpWageGapThirtyAbs{} per cent consumption ends only \genAnthConsumptionGapThirtyAbs{} per cent above baseline in the first scenario and \genZeroTfpConsumptionGapThirtyAbs{} per cent below it in the second. At a unit elasticity of substitution the wage is $(1-\gamma)Y/L$ exactly, so wages fall whenever the labour share falls faster than output per worker rises, which both scenarios deliver by construction. I also document a backward-compatible patch that gives OG-Core's capital exponent a time dimension, without which neither the phased automation path of \citet{korinek2026} nor the automation experiment of \citet{mollimas2026} is representable in the model.
\end{abstract}
