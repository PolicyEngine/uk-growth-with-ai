\begin{abstract}
\noindent I take two published artificial-intelligence scenarios to OG-UK, the UK calibration of the OG-Core overlapping-generations model, and ask how capital, labour and national income respond in general equilibrium. Both scenarios lower the labour share by \genLabourShareFallPp{} percentage points between \genStartYear{} and \genEndYear{}, the size in the ``substantial'' scenario of \citet{korinek2026}; \ShareShiftTfp{} adds that scenario's \genTfpGainPct{} per cent productivity gain and \ShareShift{} does not. With OG-UK's default capital closure ($\zetaK = \genZetaKCentral{}$), UK gross national income in \genEndYear{} is \genShareShiftTfpGniGapThirtyCentralAbs{} per cent \genShareShiftTfpGniGapThirtyCentralDir{} the baseline in \ShareShiftTfp{} and \genShareShiftGniGapThirtyCentralAbs{} per cent \genShareShiftGniGapThirtyCentralDir{} it in \ShareShift{}, against GDP gaps of \genShareShiftTfpGdpGapThirtyCentralShort{} and \genShareShiftGdpGapThirtyCentralShort{} per cent. GNI rises by less than GDP because about three-quarters of the extra capital is foreign-owned under that closure. With the capital account closed to private capital ($\zetaK = \genZetaKBound{}$) the GNI gaps are \genShareShiftTfpGniGapThirtyBoundShort{} and \genShareShiftGniGapThirtyBoundShort{} per cent. The real wage in \ShareShift{} is below the baseline under both closures. In \ShareShiftTfp{} the sign of the wage effect depends on the closure. Section~\ref{sec:incidence} sets out how the model's eighty annual cohorts give the incidence of each scenario by birth cohort and lifetime-earnings type.
\end{abstract}
